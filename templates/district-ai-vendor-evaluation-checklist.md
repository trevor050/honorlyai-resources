# District AI vendor evaluation checklist

> **Purpose:** A cross-functional question set for K-12 school districts evaluating an artificial intelligence product or service.
>
> **Canonical guide:** https://honorlyai.com/evaluate-ai-vendors-for-schools
>
> **Status:** Editable template. This is not legal advice, procurement approval, a security assessment, or a compliance certification.

## 1. Educational purpose and necessity

- [ ] The district has defined the specific educational or operational problem.
- [ ] The proposed use is more precise than "provide access to AI."
- [ ] The district has identified who benefits and what evidence would show value.
- [ ] A less data-intensive or less risky alternative was considered.
- [ ] The tool does not replace a decision or relationship that requires professional judgment without meaningful oversight.
- [ ] Excluded uses are documented.

Questions for the vendor:

1. Which K-12 use cases is the product designed to support?
2. Which use cases does the vendor discourage or prohibit?
3. What product behavior changes by grade, age, role, subject, assignment, or district setting?
4. What evidence supports the proposed use, and what are the known limitations?

## 2. Identity, access, and account governance

- [ ] Accounts can be school-managed rather than requiring unmanaged personal accounts.
- [ ] Student, teacher, administrator, and support roles are separated.
- [ ] The district can provision and deprovision users promptly.
- [ ] Authentication options match district requirements.
- [ ] Privileged access is limited, logged, and reviewable.
- [ ] The vendor explains support access and emergency access.
- [ ] The district can export necessary records before contract exit.

Ask for:

- role and permission matrix;
- single sign-on and roster integration documentation;
- administrator audit capabilities;
- account deletion and offboarding process;
- support-access controls.

## 3. Student data and privacy

- [ ] Every collected data category is listed.
- [ ] Each data category has a stated purpose.
- [ ] Optional collection can be disabled where appropriate.
- [ ] The vendor identifies whether prompts, outputs, files, metadata, identifiers, feedback, and analytics are retained.
- [ ] Model training, product improvement, human review, and secondary-use terms are explicit.
- [ ] Advertising, sale, profiling, and unrelated commercial uses are addressed.
- [ ] Retention periods and deletion methods are specific.
- [ ] The vendor can satisfy district access, correction, export, and deletion workflows where applicable.
- [ ] Subprocessors and their functions are disclosed.
- [ ] Material subprocessor changes trigger notice.
- [ ] Cross-border processing and storage locations are disclosed.

Do not accept "we are FERPA compliant" or "we are COPPA compliant" as a complete answer. Ask how the product supports the district's specific legal pathway, purpose, control, notice, consent, contract, retention, and security requirements.

## 4. AI behavior and classroom control

- [ ] Teachers can define or communicate assignment-level boundaries.
- [ ] The system can favor hints, questions, examples, and feedback when final answers are not appropriate.
- [ ] Educators receive useful visibility without requiring constant transcript surveillance.
- [ ] Automated flags are review aids, not proof of misconduct.
- [ ] The district can configure age-appropriate behavior.
- [ ] The vendor documents refusal, escalation, and high-risk-content behavior.
- [ ] Users can report inaccurate, unsafe, biased, or inappropriate output.
- [ ] Material model or policy changes are communicated.

Ask the vendor to demonstrate, not merely describe:

- a student requesting a final answer on a restricted assignment;
- a teacher changing permitted assistance;
- an administrator reviewing a concerning event;
- a false positive or inaccurate automated flag;
- an unsafe or sensitive prompt;
- a product update that changes model behavior.

## 5. Safety, security, and reliability

- [ ] The vendor provides a current security overview.
- [ ] Encryption, key management, backup, logging, monitoring, vulnerability handling, and incident response are addressed.
- [ ] Incident-notification timelines and content are contractual.
- [ ] Availability, recovery, support, and maintenance expectations are documented.
- [ ] Independent assessments are current, scoped, and available for review where appropriate.
- [ ] The district knows which claims apply to the exact product and environment being purchased.
- [ ] The vendor has a process for abuse, prompt injection, data leakage, and model-output risks.
- [ ] The district has a safe reporting path.

## 6. Accessibility, equity, and student support

- [ ] Accessibility documentation and known limitations are available.
- [ ] Keyboard, screen-reader, contrast, captioning, language, and assistive-technology needs are reviewed as applicable.
- [ ] A usable alternative exists for students who cannot or should not use the tool.
- [ ] The district has considered differential error rates and cultural or linguistic effects.
- [ ] The product does not quietly shift essential support responsibilities onto families.
- [ ] Human help remains available.

## 7. Contract and change management

- [ ] The agreement identifies the service, data, purpose, users, and term.
- [ ] District data ownership and permitted use are clear.
- [ ] Confidentiality, security, subprocessors, retention, deletion, breach notice, audit, and exit are addressed.
- [ ] Product terms cannot materially override the negotiated agreement without notice and review.
- [ ] The district can suspend or terminate use when risk becomes unacceptable.
- [ ] The vendor identifies what happens to data after termination.
- [ ] Renewal, pricing, usage limits, support, and material-change notice are documented.

## 8. Pilot readiness

- [ ] The pilot has a narrow purpose, start date, end date, cohort, and owner.
- [ ] Staff training occurs before student use.
- [ ] Family and student communication is approved.
- [ ] Stop conditions are defined.
- [ ] Evidence collection is proportionate and useful.
- [ ] Expansion is not automatic.
- [ ] The district will publish or record a decision after the pilot.
