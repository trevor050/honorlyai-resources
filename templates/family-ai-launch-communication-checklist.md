## 1. Explain the educational purpose first

The family notice should answer:

- What educational problem is the district trying to address?
- Which students and classes are included?
- Is use required, optional, or limited to particular assignments?
- What may students ask the AI to do?
- What may the AI not do?
- What remains the responsibility of the teacher?
- What decisions will not be automated?

Draft the plain-language purpose:

> `[DISTRICT NAME]` will use `[TOOL]` with `[STUDENTS / COURSES]` to support `[EXPLANATIONS / PRACTICE / FEEDBACK / OTHER]`. Teachers remain responsible for instruction, assignment rules, assessment, and decisions affecting students. The tool will not be used to `[EXCLUDED USES]`.

## 2. Describe the student experience

Include concrete examples.

Students may:

- `[ASK FOR ANOTHER EXPLANATION]`;
- `[REQUEST PRACTICE QUESTIONS]`;
- `[RECEIVE FEEDBACK UNDER TEACHER RULES]`;
- `[OTHER]`.

Students may not:

- `[REQUEST OR SUBMIT FINAL ANSWERS WHEN PROHIBITED]`;
- `[ENTER PRIVATE OR RESTRICTED INFORMATION]`;
- `[USE UNAPPROVED PERSONAL ACCOUNTS]`;
- `[OTHER]`.

Explain how students know the rule for a particular assignment and what disclosure is required.

## 3. Name the data in plain nouns

List every category relevant to the family notice:

- [ ] student name or district identifier;
- [ ] school, grade, course, and roster information;
- [ ] prompts and messages;
- [ ] uploaded assignments or course materials;
- [ ] AI responses and feedback;
- [ ] teacher instructions and assignment settings;
- [ ] usage data;
- [ ] summaries, alerts, or classifications;
- [ ] support records;
- [ ] device or diagnostic data;
- [ ] other: `[INSERT]`.

For each category, state:

- why it is needed;
- which company or service processes it;
- who at the district and vendor can access it;
- whether it is used for model training, evaluation, safety review, product improvement, advertising, or unrelated commercial purposes;
- how long it is retained;
- how it is deleted.

Avoid relying only on phrases such as “industry-standard privacy” or “we do not sell data.”

## 4. Explain teacher and administrator visibility

State exactly which views exist:

- [ ] aggregate class patterns;
- [ ] assignment activity;
- [ ] student-requested help;
- [ ] summaries;
- [ ] alerts or flags;
- [ ] individual conversation access;
- [ ] administrative implementation metrics;
- [ ] vendor support access.

Draft the visibility explanation:

> Teachers can see `[INSERT]` to support instruction, respond to safety concerns, and review possible misuse under district policy. `[ROLES]` may access individual records when `[PURPOSE / THRESHOLD]`. Access is limited by role and retained for `[PERIOD]`. The district does not use routine AI conversations to `[PROHIBITED PROFILING OR CONSEQUENCE]`.

## 5. Explain academic-integrity rules and review

Families should know:

- district approval of a tool does not make every use acceptable on every assignment;
- teachers communicate assignment-level boundaries before work begins;
- students may need to disclose material AI assistance;
- automated detector scores or AI-generated flags are not automatic proof;
- students have an opportunity to explain their process;
- existing review and appeal procedures remain available.

Policy link: `[INSERT]`

## 6. State limitations and human-help routes

The notice should say that AI can be:

- incorrect;
- incomplete;
- biased;
- inconsistent;
- inappropriate;
- unable to understand the student's full context.

Explain:

- how students are taught to verify information;
- when the tool refuses or redirects;
- how students contact a teacher or responsible adult;
- how urgent safety concerns are handled;
- how families report incorrect, harmful, inaccessible, or privacy-related behavior.

Do not promise that the service is error-free, bias-free, completely secure, or able to prevent every misuse event.

Families should know:

- where to report an issue;
- who receives the report;
- expected acknowledgment time;
- how the district preserves and reviews relevant evidence;
- when affected families are notified;
- how the district corrects data, access, safety, or instructional problems;
- where appeal or escalation occurs.

## 10. Use layered communication

### One-page notice

Include:

1. purpose and scope;
2. what students will do;
3. key data facts;
4. teacher visibility;
5. academic rules;
6. limitations and human help;
7. accessibility and alternatives;
8. contact route.

### Detailed FAQ

Include deeper answers about:

- vendor and model providers;
- data categories and subprocessors;
- retention and deletion;
- records access;
- safety and escalation;
- assignment rules and disclosure;
- accommodations and language access;
- complaints, appeals, and policy review.

### Supporting documents

Link:

- district AI policy;
- approved-tool list;
- privacy notice;
- vendor agreement or public summary where appropriate;
- board materials;
- accessibility information;
- family FAQ;
- contact and incident routes.

## 11. Pre-launch quality check

- [ ] The notice describes the classroom experience before the vendor.
- [ ] Every acronym is defined.
- [ ] Data is explained in plain nouns and verbs.
- [ ] Teacher visibility is stated directly.
- [ ] Limitations are honest.
- [ ] Consent and choice language matches district review.
- [ ] Translation and accessible formats are ready before launch.
- [ ] The contact route is monitored and tested.
- [ ] Staff answering family questions received the same facts.
- [ ] The notice has an owner, version, publication date, and review date.
