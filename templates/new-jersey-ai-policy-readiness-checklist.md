## 1. Establish board and administrative ownership

- [ ] The board-policy owner is identified.
- [ ] The superintendent or administrative implementation owner is identified.
- [ ] Technology, curriculum, privacy, legal, special services, communications, labor, records, educators, families, and students have defined review roles.
- [ ] The district distinguishes board policy from faster-changing administrative procedures.
- [ ] The policy establishes who can approve, restrict, suspend, and retire AI tools and use cases.
- [ ] The district has a review calendar and change-history process.

## 2. Create a public question and concern route

A useful local policy should tell students, parents or guardians, employees, and residents how to ask questions or raise concerns.

- [ ] A public email, form, phone number, or office is identified.
- [ ] The route is monitored by a named office.
- [ ] Expected acknowledgment and response times are stated.
- [ ] Privacy complaints, instructional concerns, accessibility issues, academic-integrity disputes, and security vulnerabilities have appropriate routing.
- [ ] Security vulnerabilities are handled confidentially rather than through a public issue.
- [ ] The district records themes and uses them in annual policy review.

## 3. Inventory current AI use before writing the rule

Inventory standalone and embedded AI in:

- learning-management systems;
- productivity and writing tools;
- search and research tools;
- tutoring and practice systems;
- assessment and academic-integrity products;
- special-education and accessibility tools;
- student-information and analytics systems;
- cybersecurity and monitoring products;
- communications, translation, and family-engagement tools;
- human-resources and administrative systems.

For each use, record:

- users and age range;
- educational or operational purpose;
- data categories;
- model provider and subprocessors;
- district approval status;
- teacher or employee control;
- family or student notice;
- accessibility review;
- contract term and next review.

## 4. Define the approved-tool and use-case process

- [ ] The process starts with a named educational or operational purpose.
- [ ] Student-facing and employee-facing uses are both covered.
- [ ] Review includes privacy, security, accessibility, instruction, contract, records, and implementation.
- [ ] The approval names users, grades, purpose, features, data, settings, term, and conditions.
- [ ] Material model, data-use, subprocessor, or feature changes trigger re-review.
- [ ] Teachers are not expected to perform district-level vendor review alone.
- [ ] Unreviewed tools have a temporary status and a route for review.
- [ ] The approved-tool list is maintained and visible to staff.

Use the [District AI vendor evaluation checklist](district-ai-vendor-evaluation-checklist.md) and [District AI procurement question bank](district-ai-procurement-question-bank.md).

## 5. Preserve employee oversight and professional judgment

The policy should identify decisions that require human responsibility.

- [ ] Teachers remain responsible for instruction and assessment.
- [ ] Administrators remain responsible for discipline and consequential decisions.
- [ ] Special-services professionals remain responsible for disability-related decisions.
- [ ] Counselors and qualified staff remain responsible for student support and crisis response.
- [ ] Employees verify material factual, legal, financial, personnel, safety, and student-specific output.
- [ ] Automated flags, detector scores, risk labels, and recommendations are reviewed as uncertain evidence.
- [ ] Employees know how to override, correct, report, and escalate AI output.

## 6. Establish classroom and assignment rules

- [ ] The district provides common AI-use levels or vocabulary.
- [ ] Teachers communicate assignment-specific rules before work begins.
- [ ] Allowed and prohibited actions use concrete verbs.
- [ ] Approved tools and accounts are named.
- [ ] Disclosure expectations match the amount of permitted assistance.
- [ ] Students remain responsible for accuracy and explanation.
- [ ] Accommodations and language-access supports are preserved.
- [ ] Suspected misuse receives human review and appeal.

Use the [Classroom AI use levels](classroom-ai-use-levels.md), [Assignment AI-use statement builder](assignment-ai-use-statement-builder.md), and [AI detector due-process checklist](ai-detector-due-process-checklist.md).

## 7. Address equity, accessibility, and language access

Review whether implementation affects:

- students with disabilities;
- English learners and multilingual families;
- students without reliable devices or connectivity;
- students whose cultural or linguistic patterns may be misread by automated systems;
- students who cannot or should not use a required AI service;
- families needing translated, accessible, or non-digital communication.

Confirm:

- [ ] accessibility documentation and hands-on testing are complete;
- [ ] required accommodations remain available;
- [ ] a meaningful non-AI alternative exists;
- [ ] language quality and family communication are reviewed;
- [ ] subgroup outcomes and error patterns are examined proportionately;
- [ ] accessibility and equity issues can block or condition approval.

## 8. Address student data and existing law

A new AI policy does not replace existing federal and New Jersey student-data obligations.

- [ ] Data categories and purposes are documented.
- [ ] Prompts, files, outputs, summaries, alerts, and derived data are included.
- [ ] Model training, evaluation, product improvement, human review, advertising, profiling, sale, and unrelated uses are explicit.
- [ ] District control, access, retention, deletion, export, and incident terms are documented.
- [ ] Subprocessors and material changes are reviewed.
- [ ] Parent, eligible-student, records, and legal-demand procedures are addressed where applicable.
- [ ] District counsel reviewed the intended legal pathway and current New Jersey requirements.

Use the [School AI privacy review checklist](school-ai-privacy-review-checklist.md) and [AI data lifecycle worksheet](ai-data-lifecycle-worksheet.md).

## 9. Prepare public-facing communication

The district website should make it easy to find:

- [ ] adopted board policy;
- [ ] administrative implementation guidance;
- [ ] approved-tool list or process;
- [ ] family notice and FAQ;
- [ ] student and staff expectations;
- [ ] privacy and accessibility information;
- [ ] public question route;
- [ ] incident or complaint route;
- [ ] policy owner, approval date, version, and next review date.

Use the [Family AI launch communication checklist](family-ai-launch-communication-checklist.md) and [Parent and family AI notice](parent-family-ai-notice.md).

## 10. Define incident and suspension authority

- [ ] The district can pause a feature, use case, or product.
- [ ] Privacy, security, safety, accessibility, academic-integrity, and instructional incidents have owners.
- [ ] Evidence preservation and student privacy are addressed.
- [ ] Vendor-notification and contractual timelines are known.
- [ ] Affected people receive appropriate notice.
- [ ] Corrective action, root-cause review, and return-to-service approval are documented.
- [ ] Material incidents trigger policy or tool re-review.

Use the [AI incident response plan](ai-incident-response-plan.md).

## 11. Schedule annual and event-driven review

Annual review should examine:

- legal and NJDOE changes;
- public questions and complaints;
- approved-tool inventory;
- vendor and model changes;
- incidents and near misses;
- accessibility and equity evidence;
- academic-integrity cases;
- pilot and learning evidence;
- teacher workload and support burden;
- family and student communication;
- policy language that proved unclear.

Event-driven review triggers:

- [ ] material legal or regulatory change;
- [ ] model or subprocessor change;
- [ ] new data use;
- [ ] significant privacy or security incident;
- [ ] harmful or discriminatory output pattern;
- [ ] accessibility barrier;
- [ ] new consequential use;
- [ ] major contract renewal;
- [ ] public or board direction.
