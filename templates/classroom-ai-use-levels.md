# Classroom AI use levels for K-12 assignments

> **Canonical guide:** https://honorlyai.com/ai-use-levels-for-schools
>
> **Direct answer:** Put one AI use level on every assignment where ambiguity is likely. Then name the permitted actions, prohibited actions, and required evidence.

## Level 1: No AI

**Student direction:** Complete this assignment without generative AI assistance.

Appropriate when the teacher needs direct evidence of an unaided skill, foundational knowledge, live performance, or independent reasoning.

Usually not allowed:

- brainstorming with AI;
- AI-generated outlines;
- rewriting or paraphrasing;
- AI feedback on the response;
- AI-generated code, calculations, translations, answers, or citations.

Teacher should clarify whether ordinary tools such as spellcheck, text-to-speech, calculators, dictionaries, or approved accommodations remain allowed.

### Assignment label

> **AI Use Level 1: No AI.** Do not use generative AI for any part of this assignment. Approved accommodations and tools: `[LIST]`.

## Level 2: AI for preparation, not submitted content

**Student direction:** AI may help you prepare, study, question, or plan, but it may not create language, code, media, analysis, or answers that you submit.

Possible allowed uses:

- generating practice questions;
- asking for a concept explanation;
- brainstorming topics before selecting one independently;
- building a study plan;
- checking understanding through a quiz;
- identifying vocabulary to review.

Required evidence may include notes, an independent outline, a planning reflection, or a brief disclosure.

### Assignment label

> **AI Use Level 2: Preparation only.** You may use AI for `[ALLOWED USES]`. Do not include AI-generated content in the submitted work. Submit `[PROCESS EVIDENCE]`.

## Level 3: AI collaboration with disclosure

**Student direction:** AI may assist defined parts of the process. You must disclose use, verify output, preserve your own reasoning, and remain able to explain the final work.

Possible allowed uses:

- feedback on a student-created draft;
- alternative examples or approaches;
- debugging student-written code;
- critique against a rubric;
- organization or revision suggestions;
- language support within stated boundaries.

Teacher should specify whether AI may generate text, code, images, audio, citations, or calculations and how much may appear in the final submission.

### Assignment label

> **AI Use Level 3: Collaboration with disclosure.** AI may assist with `[ALLOWED PARTS]`. It may not `[PROHIBITED PARTS]`. Submit `[PROMPTS/OUTPUTS/REVISION NOTES/DISCLOSURE]`. You must be able to explain and defend the final work.

## Level 4: AI-integrated task

**Student direction:** AI use is an expected part of the assignment. Your judgment, prompting, verification, revision, comparison, and reflection are part of what is assessed.

Possible task designs:

- compare multiple model responses;
- identify and correct factual or reasoning errors;
- revise a weak AI draft using disciplinary knowledge;
- document how constraints changed output;
- audit an AI response for bias, evidence, safety, or accessibility;
- combine AI-assisted exploration with independent analysis.

### Assignment label

> **AI Use Level 4: AI-integrated.** Use `[APPROVED TOOL/WORKFLOW]` to `[PURPOSE]`. Submit `[PROCESS EVIDENCE]`. Your grade will evaluate `[JUDGMENT, VERIFICATION, REVISION, REFLECTION, AND SUBJECT KNOWLEDGE]`, not merely the generated output.

## Teacher planning checklist

Before assigning a level, answer:

- What skill or understanding am I assessing?
- Which AI assistance would preserve that assessment?
- Which assistance would replace it?
- Are approved tools and accounts available equitably?
- What accessibility tools remain allowed?
- What evidence of process is proportionate?
- How should students disclose use?
- How will I respond to uncertainty without treating detection output as proof?
- What should a student do when AI gives unsafe, private, biased, or inaccurate output?
