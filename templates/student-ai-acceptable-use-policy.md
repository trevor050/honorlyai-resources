## 1. Purpose

`[DISTRICT NAME]` uses approved artificial intelligence tools to support learning, creativity, feedback, practice, accessibility, and responsible technology literacy. AI does not replace student thinking, teacher judgment, or the student's responsibility for submitted work.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies when students use AI:

- through district accounts, devices, networks, or approved applications;
- for district coursework, activities, or services;
- with school information or another person's information;
- when a teacher or assignment refers to this policy.

## 3. Follow the assignment's AI use level

Students must follow the teacher's stated AI use level and assignment directions. When directions are unclear, students should ask before using AI.

- **Level 1, No AI:** Complete the work without generative AI assistance.
- **Level 2, AI for preparation:** AI may support brainstorming, questions, vocabulary, study, or planning, but may not create submitted content.
- **Level 3, AI collaboration with disclosure:** AI may assist defined parts of the work when the student documents use and remains responsible for reasoning, verification, and revision.
- **Level 4, AI-integrated task:** AI use is expected and evaluated as part of the assignment, including the student's judgment, process, verification, and reflection.

See the full [classroom AI use levels](classroom-ai-use-levels.md).

## 4. Allowed use

When permitted by the assignment, students may use approved AI tools to:

- ask for explanations, examples, practice questions, or feedback;
- brainstorm or organize ideas;
- compare approaches;
- identify gaps in reasoning;
- practice a skill;
- revise work while preserving the student's own voice and understanding;
- use approved accessibility or language supports;
- explore how AI systems behave and where they fail.

## 5. Prohibited use

Students may not:

- use AI when the assignment forbids it;
- submit AI-generated work as their own;
- bypass a teacher's stated boundary or product safeguard;
- enter personal, confidential, sensitive, or identifying information unless specifically authorized through an approved workflow;
- upload another person's work, image, voice, record, or information without permission and authorization;
- use AI to harass, threaten, impersonate, discriminate, sexualize, exploit, deceive, or invade privacy;
- generate or distribute harmful, illegal, or school-prohibited content;
- use AI to gain unauthorized access, expose credentials, evade security, or disrupt systems;
- rely on AI output as verified fact, professional advice, or an authoritative source;
- fabricate citations, evidence, observations, data, interviews, or experiences;
- use automated detection or scoring output to accuse another person.

## 6. Student responsibility

Students remain responsible for:

- understanding and explaining submitted work;
- checking facts, calculations, sources, quotations, and citations;
- identifying harmful, biased, unsafe, or inappropriate output;
- preserving drafts, prompts, outputs, notes, or revision history when the teacher requires process evidence;
- disclosing AI assistance in the format required by the assignment;
- stopping and asking for help when the system behaves unexpectedly.

## 7. Disclosure statement

When disclosure is required, students should identify:

1. the tool used;
2. the date or stage of use;
3. what the tool helped with;
4. what content or reasoning the student changed, rejected, verified, or created independently.

Suggested format:

> I used `[TOOL]` to `[PURPOSE]`. I reviewed the output by `[VERIFICATION OR REVISION]`. The final decisions and submitted work are my responsibility.

## 8. Privacy and safety

Students should use district-approved accounts and tools for schoolwork. Students should not enter information that their teacher or district has not authorized, including passwords, private contact information, health information, disciplinary information, unpublished student work, or information identifying another person.

A student who sees unexpected personal information, unsafe content, or unauthorized access should stop, preserve only the minimum evidence necessary, and report it to `[CONTACT]`.

## 9. Teacher and district visibility

When students use district-approved AI services, authorized educators and administrators may review relevant activity for instructional support, safety, policy implementation, troubleshooting, and other approved school purposes. The district should explain the scope of visibility and access in its privacy notices and product-specific documentation.

## 10. Mistakes, support, and response

A policy response should consider age, intent, impact, prior instruction, accessibility, the assignment's clarity, and whether the student sought help. The goal should include teaching responsible use, correcting harm, protecting privacy and safety, and preserving academic integrity.

Potential responses may include:

- clarification and reteaching;
- revision or reassessment;
- reflection or process documentation;
- temporary restriction of an AI feature;
- family communication;
- support or safety referral;
- consequences under existing district policy when warranted.

Automated flags or AI detectors should not be treated as conclusive proof by themselves.
