## Measurement-card instructions

For each metric, identify:

1. the definition;
2. why it matters to the pilot decision;
3. the baseline or comparison;
4. the data source;
5. the owner;
6. the collection schedule;
7. subgroup or accessibility analysis;
8. privacy and retention limits;
9. target and warning threshold;
10. action when the threshold is crossed.

## Metric 7: safety performance

Track:

- harmful or age-inappropriate responses;
- self-harm, threat, abuse, or crisis-related events;
- biased or discriminatory output;
- incorrect refusal or overblocking;
- missed events;
- alert false positives;
- time to human review;
- outcome and follow-up.

Do not publish identifying student details in the pilot report.

## Metric 8: privacy and access-control events

Track:

- sensitive information entered unnecessarily;
- unauthorized access;
- account sharing;
- incorrect role assignment;
- unexpected data flow or subprocessor use;
- retention or deletion failure;
- support access outside the approved process;
- parent, student, or employee complaints.

## Metric 12: stakeholder trust

Ask students, teachers, families, and administrators whether they understand:

- what the tool is for;
- which uses are allowed;
- what data is collected;
- what adults can see;
- how to report a problem;
- how automated output is reviewed;
- whether a meaningful alternative exists.

Avoid reducing trust to a single satisfaction score. Record major themes, disagreements, and unanswered questions.
