# K-12 AI governance terminology

These definitions describe how terms are used in this toolkit. Districts should align final language with their own policies and applicable law.

## Artificial intelligence system

A machine-based system that produces outputs such as predictions, recommendations, classifications, text, images, audio, code, or decisions from provided inputs and learned or programmed methods.

## Generative AI

An AI system that creates new content, including text, images, audio, video, code, or combinations of those formats.

## Teacher-controlled AI

A classroom AI approach in which educators define learning context and acceptable assistance, receive useful visibility into relevant student activity, and retain authority to intervene or change the workflow.

## District-governed AI

AI deployed through school-approved identity, privacy, access, policy, procurement, support, and accountability processes rather than unmanaged personal use.

## Learning-first support

AI assistance designed to advance student understanding through explanations, questions, practice, examples, and feedback instead of automatically replacing the thinking or performance being assessed.

## AI use level

A short assignment label that tells students how much AI assistance is permitted, what uses are allowed, and what disclosure or evidence is required.

## Human oversight

Meaningful review or decision-making by an authorized person who has enough information, authority, time, and training to understand the context and act.

## Student data

Information connected or reasonably linkable to a student. The legal treatment of particular information depends on context and applicable law, so districts should use precise definitions from their policies and agreements.

## Education record

A term defined by the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act and its regulations. Do not assume every data element is or is not an education record without district-specific analysis.

## Purpose limitation

Using information only for the stated, approved purpose rather than repurposing it merely because it is available.

## Data minimization

Collecting, using, disclosing, and retaining only the information reasonably necessary for the approved purpose.

## Subprocessor

A third party engaged by a vendor to process data in support of the service. District review should address identity, function, location, security, contract flow-down, and change notice.

## Automated flag

A system-generated signal that an interaction may require review. A flag is not proof of misconduct, risk, intent, or factual accuracy.

## AI incident

An event involving unexpected system behavior, unauthorized access, inappropriate disclosure, unsafe or harmful output, material policy violation, or another condition requiring review and response.

## Pilot

A bounded implementation with a defined purpose, participating group, duration, support plan, evidence plan, stop conditions, and decision date.
