# Project governance

The HonorlyAI K-12 AI Governance Toolkit is maintained by HonorlyAI LLC as a public educational resource.

## Maintainer responsibilities

Maintainers are responsible for:

- reviewing proposed corrections and improvements;
- keeping canonical HonorlyAI links aligned with public site routes;
- identifying the date and source of material legal or policy updates;
- protecting the repository from private, identifying, confidential, or security-sensitive content;
- preserving clear licensing and attribution;
- favoring useful, evidence-backed resources over search-engine filler.

## Decision principles

Changes are evaluated according to:

1. usefulness to real K-12 decision-makers;
2. accuracy and source quality;
3. student privacy and safety;
4. classroom practicality;
5. accessibility and plain language;
6. ease of local adaptation;
7. consistency with the repository's scope and license.

## Source hierarchy

When sources conflict or age differently, the project generally prefers:

1. controlling law and regulation;
2. current official agency guidance;
3. current state or local authority materials;
4. consensus standards and frameworks;
5. peer-reviewed research;
6. well-documented implementation evidence;
7. secondary commentary.

A template may deliberately present a conservative review question without claiming that one answer is legally required.

## Versioning

Material releases use semantic-style versions:

- **Major:** structural changes or materially different policy assumptions;
- **Minor:** new resources or substantial expansions;
- **Patch:** corrections, wording improvements, and link maintenance.

The repository changelog records notable changes. Individual district adaptations should maintain their own owner, approval date, review date, and change history.

## Independence and endorsement

A contribution, citation, fork, or adaptation does not imply that HonorlyAI endorses a person, district, vendor, product, policy, or legal interpretation.
