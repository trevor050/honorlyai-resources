# School AI pilots, tutoring, and family communication

> **Direct answer:** A responsible school AI rollout should test a narrow educational workflow, measure learning and operational consequences, evaluate whether the system actually tutors, and explain the implementation to families before student use.

This hub connects four canonical HonorlyAI field notes with editable implementation resources.

## 1. Use a pilot to make a decision

A pilot should test whether the district can govern, support, teach with, and explain a particular workflow. It should not exist only to produce logins or enthusiastic quotes.

Read [How to run a six-week school AI pilot that produces a real decision](https://honorlyai.com/blog/six-week-school-ai-pilot) for a week-by-week implementation plan.

Use:

- [School district AI pilot plan](../templates/school-district-ai-pilot-plan.md)
- [School AI pilot measurement scorecard](../templates/school-ai-pilot-measurement-scorecard.md)
- [AI incident response plan](../templates/ai-incident-response-plan.md)
- [Parent and family AI notice](../templates/parent-family-ai-notice.md)

A final pilot decision should be one of four things: expand within scope, expand with conditions, revise and re-pilot, or stop.

## 2. Measure more than usage

Logins, prompts, and minutes can describe activity. They do not establish learning, instructional value, safety, fairness, or sustainability.

Read [What should a school AI pilot measure? 12 metrics that matter](https://honorlyai.com/blog/school-ai-pilot-metrics) for a balanced framework covering:

- learning progress and independent transfer;
- quality of student help-seeking;
- teacher workload and instructional actionability;
- academic-integrity, safety, and privacy events;
- accessibility, equity, and reliability;
- support burden, adoption, total cost, and trust.

The [School AI pilot measurement scorecard](../templates/school-ai-pilot-measurement-scorecard.md) provides a measurement card and decision table for each metric.

## 3. Distinguish tutoring from answer vending

A system is not an educational tutor merely because it gives a correct response. Tutoring requires diagnosis, scaffolding, checks for understanding, adaptation to relevant context, and a path back to the teacher.

Read [How to evaluate an AI tutor: learning support versus answer vending](https://honorlyai.com/blog/how-to-evaluate-ai-tutor-schools) for an educator-led evaluation rubric.

A rubric describes the behavior a district wants. It does not explain what has to be true inside a product for that behavior to hold under pressure. [Introducing Honorly Intelligence: rebuilding the brain behind the tutor](https://honorlyai.com/blog/honorly-intelligence) is a worked example of one vendor answering that question in public: which decisions are left to a language model, which are enforced deterministically by the surrounding application, and how the two are separated so a persuasive student cannot talk the system out of a classroom rule. Districts can ask any vendor for the same account.

Use the [Teacher visibility and AI tutor review worksheet](../templates/teacher-visibility-ai-tutor-review.md) to test whether the service:

- asks what the student tried;
- responds to the actual misconception;
- gives hints and questions before full solutions;
- checks independent understanding;
- aligns with assignment rules and course materials;
- explains uncertainty and escalates appropriately;
- provides proportional teacher visibility;
- supports accessibility and language needs.

## 4. Explain the launch before students encounter it

Family communication is part of governance. A district should be able to explain the purpose, approved users, student experience, data categories, teacher visibility, retention, academic rules, limitations, alternatives, and question route in plain language.

Read [How districts should explain classroom AI to parents before launch](https://honorlyai.com/blog/explain-classroom-ai-to-parents) for a layered notice model.

Use:

- [Family AI launch communication checklist](../templates/family-ai-launch-communication-checklist.md)
- [Parent and family AI notice](../templates/parent-family-ai-notice.md)
- [School AI privacy review checklist](../templates/school-ai-privacy-review-checklist.md)

Avoid absolute assurances such as “the AI is always accurate,” “no data can ever be exposed,” or “the product is legally compliant.” Explain safeguards and limitations honestly.

## Suggested implementation sequence

1. Define the educational job and excluded uses.
2. Complete privacy, security, accessibility, legal, and instructional review.
3. Select representative teachers and a narrow student cohort.
4. Establish baseline evidence and stop conditions.
5. Train teachers and students on the exact workflow.
6. Publish family notice and question routes.
7. Run real assignments and record incidents and support burden.
8. Test edge cases intentionally.
9. Compare evidence with the pre-defined decision thresholds.
10. Publish a plain-language outcome and next review date.
